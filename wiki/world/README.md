# World blockout: every area laid out before the art

The owner's plan (2026-11-02): build the whole world conceptually first, then fill in the art.

1. **Blockout (done).** Every town, route, dungeon, interior and new planet is laid out on a cell grid
   with placeholder tiles, and every overworld area is placed on one connected world map (`_world.png`,
   `<planet>/_planet.png`). This layout is kept as drafted; sizes may grow later for big structures.
2. **Greybox (now).** Every piece as a simple 3D model in placeholder colours, one category at a time
   ([`tools/greybox/`](../../tools/greybox/README.md)). Rendered pieces land in `greybox/`.
3. **Art (last).** Real pixel art per piece, tracked by the same rows.

## Files

- `<planet>/<area>.yaml`: one area. The format is documented at the top of
  [`tools/blockout/blockout/area.py`](../../tools/blockout/blockout/area.py).
- `<planet>/<area>.png`: its review sheet (vanilla map it replaces | blockout | numbered legend).
- `CONNECTIONS.csv` and `GUIDE.md`: the area graph, computed by `python3 -m blockout guide` (never edited by hand).
  Edges come from each area's exits, doors and warps, its `travel:` list (ships, shuttles, tides, dives and
  scripted moves that are not on the map), and vanilla's dive connections and script warps. The guide also
  checks that every area can be reached from Mos Pelgo.
- `INDEX.csv`: every area and every placed piece, generated by `python3 -m blockout index`. Each piece
  links to its row in [`../mapping/world_art.csv`](../mapping/world_art.csv) by `art` id and carries that
  row's `art_status` and `tool`.
- `<planet>/_planet_tiles.png`: the planet's overworld assembled from the greybox terrain renders at 16 px per
  cell, dungeons in rows below (`python3 -m blockout world-tiles`, which also writes `_world_tiles.png`, every
  planet in story order at half size; that file is not committed, 13 MB).
- `greybox/`: rendered greybox pieces (`<planet>_<name>.png`), kit sheets (`_kit-*.png`) and areas in relief
  (`areas/`), written by `tools/greybox`.

Status: an area's `status` is its layout (`TODO`, `BLOCKOUT`, `APPROVED`); its `art_status` is
`pending-mockup`, `pending-final-art`, `art-complete` or `imported-in-game`, and its `notes` say what it needs
drawn. `python3 -m blockout todo` lists areas and pieces by status, planet, kind or tool; `--set` records
progress. The wiki is the one list: no separate to-do docs.

## Terrain legend (one character per cell)

| Char | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `.` | open ground |
| `:` | path, street or plaza (the signal the player follows) |
| `"` | wild encounter ground (tall grass, scrub, ash, ferns) |
| `~` | water (surf) |
| `=` | raised walkway: bridge, tree platform, catwalk |
| `H` | ladder or stairs between levels |
| `T` | edge nature that blocks (trees, rocks) |
| `#` | cliff, wall or rock face |
| `v ^ < >` | ledge, jump down in that direction |
| `_` / `\|` | indoor floor / indoor wall |
| `x` | blocked, not yet decided (left over from a vanilla import) |

## Rules

- **Each planet is its own map (owner, 2026-11-02).** Walkable borders between planets are cut; ships
  link them (hangars, docking bays, shuttles) along the planet graph in `../mapping/hyperlanes.csv`: one row
  per edge, naming the overworld area and ship room at each end and the unlock. A hangar is a building
  on the main road at the edge of the planet that faces the destination (where the vanilla road left the map),
  with a sign beside its door, so the player walks straight into it. A ship room's StarNAV console lists
  the unlocked edges from that room. `blockout check` fails if an edge lacks a door, a way back or a sign,
  or a planet has no edge; `blockout guide` lists the graph and the badges held on first landing. `blockout layout <PLANET>` fits a planet's towns, routes and
  landmarks together from their exits and writes each area's `pos`; it reports exits with no way back,
  loops that do not close and overlaps.
- Towns can be bigger and any shape (owner, 2026-11-02): GBA storage is no longer the limit, but walks
  must stay short. Optional side areas are welcome; they use the characters already in the wiki.
- `replaces` names the vanilla map an area is built into (its doors, trainers and scripts come from
  there). The size, shape and exits can change.
- `~` is each planet's surf medium: water on most planets, the salt lake on Tatooine.
- Signal and noise (`../bible/WORLD_ART.md`): paths stay clear; props sit at edges, corners and dead space.
- `python3 -m blockout check` must report no problems before an area is sent for review. Redrawn towns and
  new areas must also pass the reach check (every exit, door and warp reachable on foot or by surf).
  Imported routes and caves skip it, because their stairs, bridges and stacked rooms are vanilla geometry
  the grid does not model.

## How each area was made

- Every vanilla map was imported (`blockout import-all`) as a `TODO` starting point: terrain from its
  collision (`T` where vanilla is green, `#` otherwise), its trainers (names from `trainers.csv`), items,
  signs, doors and connections.
- **Towns** are redrawn from scratch: new shapes, more buildings, Star Wars landmarks.
- **Routes and caves** keep vanilla's walkable shape (it is already well paced), with vanilla houses turned
  into buildings, cross-planet edges cut, and planet props placed at the edges of open ground without
  blocking any path.
- **New optional areas** (`kind: landmark`, `replaces: new`) hang off a route by a side exit.
